## 1. Purpose and when to use this script

Use this script when a member calls with questions about how their prescription drug coverage works, what a drug will cost, or which pharmacy to use. It applies to members enrolled in Sample Health Plan Medicare Advantage Prescription Drug (MAPD) plans and stand-alone Prescription Drug Plans (PDP).

This script is educational. Advocates must not recommend a specific drug, change a prescription, or give clinical advice. Clinical questions are transferred to a pharmacist (see Section 13).

Lines marked **[VERBATIM – LEGAL]** must be read exactly as written. Do not paraphrase, shorten, or reorder them. All other lines are guidance and may be said in the advocate's own words.

---

## 2. Opening

"Thank you for calling Sample Health Plan Pharmacy Services. My name is [Advocate first name]. I can help you understand your prescription drug coverage today. May I have your name, please?"

*Guidance:* Speak at a relaxed pace. Many callers are older adults who may be anxious about costs. Confirm you are speaking with the member or an authorized representative before discussing any plan details.

---

## 3. Identity verification

**[VERBATIM – LEGAL]**
"Before we continue, I need to verify your identity to protect your health information. Please tell me your full name, your date of birth, and the member ID number shown on your Sample Health Plan card."

*Guidance:* Verify all three items against the member record. If the caller is not the member, check the record for an Authorized Representative or a Personal Representative form on file. If none is on file, you may give only general, non-member-specific information.

If verification fails twice, say: "I'm sorry, I'm not able to verify your information. For your protection, I can only share general information on this call." Then continue with general education only.

## 5. Call purpose

"How can I help you with your prescriptions today?"

*Guidance:* Listen for the main reason for the call. Common reasons:
- Cost of a specific drug
- Why a drug costs more than expected
- Whether a drug is covered (on the formulary)
- Prior authorization or step therapy
- Switching to mail order
- Extra Help (LIS)

Before quoting any cost, read the pricing disclaimer in Section 6.

---

## 6. Pricing disclaimer

**[VERBATIM – LEGAL]**
"Before I share any prices, please know that your cost may vary depending on your plan, the pharmacy you use, the drug payment stage you are in, and whether you qualify for Extra Help. Prices and coverage are subject to change and are confirmed when your prescription is filled."

*Guidance:* Read this disclaimer **once per call, before the first price quote**, even if the member says they have heard it before. Note in the call record that the disclaimer was read (disposition flag `DISC-RX`).

---

## 7. Drug tiers

"Your plan places covered drugs into tiers. In general, lower tiers cost less."

*Guidance:* Use the table below to explain tiers. Copay amounts are illustrative for the 2027 plan year at a preferred retail pharmacy for a 30-day supply. Always look up the member's actual plan in the benefits tool before quoting.

| Tier | Name | Typical drugs | Illustrative copay (preferred retail, 30-day) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Preferred Generic | Common generics | $0 |
| 2 | Generic | Other generics | $5 |
| 3 | Preferred Brand | Preferred brand-name drugs and some higher-cost generics | $47 |
| 4 | Non-Preferred Drug | Non-preferred brand and generic drugs | 40% coinsurance |
| 5 | Specialty | High-cost specialty drugs | 25% coinsurance |

If the member's drug is not on the formulary, say: "That drug is not on your plan's drug list. Your prescriber can ask for an exception, or may be able to suggest a covered alternative." Do not suggest a specific alternative drug.

---

## 8. Generic and brand-name drugs

"Generic drugs have the same active ingredients as their brand-name versions and are approved by the FDA. They usually cost less."

*Guidance:* If the member asks whether they should switch to a generic, say: "That's a great question for your doctor or pharmacist. They can tell you whether a generic is right for you." Do not advise switching.

---

## 9. Coverage rules: prior authorization, step therapy, quantity limits

Some drugs have rules that must be met before the plan will cover them. Explain in plain language:

- **Prior authorization (PA):** "Your doctor needs to get approval from the plan before we cover this drug."
- **Step therapy (ST):** "The plan asks that you try a lower-cost drug first. If it doesn't work for you, your doctor can request the original drug."
- **Quantity limits (QL):** "The plan covers up to a certain amount of this drug over a set period."

*Guidance:* If a PA is pending, look up the status in the PA tracker and give the member the status only. Standard PA decisions are made within 72 hours; expedited decisions within 24 hours after the plan receives the prescriber's supporting statement. Do not promise an outcome.

---

## 10. Pharmacy options

"You can fill prescriptions at any pharmacy in our network, but you'll usually pay less at a preferred pharmacy."

- **Preferred retail pharmacies:** lowest copays at retail locations.
- **Standard retail pharmacies:** in network, but copays are usually higher than at preferred pharmacies.
- **SampleRx Mail Pharmacy (mail order):** "Mail order can be a convenient option for medicines you take regularly. You can get up to a 100-day supply, and standard shipping is free."

*Guidance:* Mail-order delivery usually takes **5 to 7 business days** after the order is processed. Advise members to order a refill when they have about 14 days of medicine left. To enroll in mail order, transfer to SampleRx Mail Pharmacy or direct the member to the member portal.

Out-of-network pharmacies are covered only in limited situations, such as an emergency while traveling. Refer these questions to Section 13.

---

## 11. Drug payment stages

"How much you pay can change during the year, depending on which drug payment stage you are in."

*Guidance:* Explain the three stages for the 2027 plan year:

1. **Deductible stage:** The member pays the full negotiated cost of certain drugs until the deductible is met. Some plans have no deductible for Tier 1 and Tier 2 drugs.
2. **Initial coverage stage:** The member pays their copay or coinsurance; the plan pays the rest.
3. **Catastrophic coverage stage:** After the member's out-of-pocket drug costs reach the annual limit (**$2,100, illustrative**), the member pays **$0** for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the calendar year.

Do **not** refer to a "coverage gap" or "donut hole." The coverage gap no longer applies under current Part D rules. If the member mentions it, say: "The coverage gap, sometimes called the donut hole, no longer applies. Once you reach the yearly out-of-pocket limit, you pay nothing more for covered drugs that year."

**Medicare Prescription Payment Plan:** Members can choose to spread their out-of-pocket drug costs into monthly payments across the year. Transfer interested members to Billing Services.

---

## 12. Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy, LIS)

"Medicare has a program called Extra Help that can lower prescription costs for people with limited income and resources."

*Guidance:* If the member may qualify, tell them they can apply through Social Security at ssa.gov or by calling Social Security. Do not determine eligibility. If the member's record shows an LIS level, use the LIS copay schedule in the benefits tool when quoting costs.

---

## 13. Escalation and questions you cannot answer

Transfer or escalate as follows:

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Clinical question (side effects, interactions, dosing) | Warm transfer to Clinical Pharmacist line |
| Formulary exception or coverage determination request | Transfer to Coverage Determinations |
| Premium billing or payment questions | Transfer to Insurance / Billing Services |
| Complaint or grievance | Follow the Grievance Intake procedure |
| Out-of-network or emergency fill | Transfer to Pharmacy Help Desk |

If no approved answer exists for the member's question, do not guess. Say: "I want to make sure I give you the right information. Let me connect you with someone who can help," and log the question with disposition code `RX-NOANS` so Knowledge Operations can review it.
