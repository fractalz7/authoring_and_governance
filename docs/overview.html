Knowledge & Search Platform · Use case 3 · Proposal and proof of concept

Author it once, govern it once, and give every channel the same answer

Humana's answers live in hundreds of unclassified documents, and the same legal wording drifts across scripts, bots and the web. This proposal turns documents into governed knowledge units: the AI extracts, tags, matches and flags; Legal and Knowledge Ops decide; and only approved units reach search and channels.

Key tradeoff

Governance before coverage. Search goes live only on approved units, starting with one domain, so early on it answers fewer questions than a search over every raw file would. In return, every answer is current, cited and approved, and every question it cannot answer becomes the next unit to write. The cost is a review queue that needs staff and turnaround targets.

The problem, and who we built for

The failure shows up on a live call, but it starts upstream: the unit of governance is the document, while the unit of risk is a single sentence. We focus on the people who can fix it at the source.

Main persona

Maya, Knowledge Ops author

Maintains a few hundred scripts and articles across Pharmacy and Insurance.

  • When Legal changes wording, she has to find every copy from memory and keyword search
  • Near-copies with slightly different wording are missed
  • No view of what advocates searched for and could not find
Gatekeeper

David, Legal and Compliance approver

Owns mandatory verbatim; accountable for what members hear.

  • Approves whole documents to check one paragraph
  • Cannot see where approved wording ended up, or how it was changed
  • Finds drift in audits, after the harm
Feels the pain

Advocates

Search several tools, get documents not answers, find two wordings of the same disclaimer and cannot tell which Legal approved, with a member waiting.

Consumers

Channel and AI teams

Voice bot, chat and web each rebuild their own answers from raw documents, so each gets a slightly different "truth".

Bears the cost

Members

Different answers from the bot, the advocate and the website: repeat calls, lost trust, and sometimes a wrong answer about drug costs.

Should AI build the graph from entities and relationships?

Yes, for the facts; with people deciding the shape. There are two graphs, and AI plays a different part in each.

Graph 1 · the schema

The ontology itself

Classes and relation types: Knowledge Unit, Topic, Plan, "applies to", "supersedes".

  • AI proposes it bottom-up from the entities and relations it finds
  • SMEs and Legal approve every class and relation type
  • Changes slowly; versioned like code
Graph 2 · the instances

The knowledge graph

Real entities and links: unit RX§6 is about pricing, applies to MAPD 2027, approved under LGL-2026-118.

  • AI extracts entities and relations from every unit, at scale
  • Extraction is constrained to the approved schema
  • Anything outside the schema becomes a proposal, not a fact
Recommendation

Schema first, then guided extraction

  • Free extraction invents relation types ("is kind of related to"); a fixed schema keeps the graph queryable
  • Resolve entities against the lexicon so "MAPD" and "Medicare Advantage Rx" become one node
  • Measure extraction against a sample SMEs label, before trusting the graph
  • Governance relations (approved by, can read) are set by people, never extracted

Phases 2 and 3, then improve as data comes in

Start with people deciding and the system proposing; widen automation only where the evaluations show it is safe.

  1. Phase 1 · built as a proof of concept

    Author and govern, one domain

    Pharmacy (Rx) scripts, where inconsistent legal wording costs most.

    • Units extracted and tagged against the ontology
    • Duplicate, drift and conflict check
    • Human approval gates with a role check
    • Versioned publish and an audit log
  2. Phase 2

    Search and drift on governed units

    Search goes live with the first governed knowledge base, not after a full migration.

    • Hybrid search over published units only, scoped by role before retrieval
    • Every answer cites its unit; "no approved answer" when it has none
    • Unanswered questions land in the author's queue with a proposed unit
    • Golden set measured before anyone relies on it
    • Revised source documents flag their units stale and propose the fix
  3. Phase 3

    Channels and scale

    The other AI use cases plug into the same approved units.

    • One API for advocate assist, member chat, voice bot, web and outbound
    • More domains: Insurance, then clinical with a clinical reviewer gate
    • Bulk migration with AI splitting unmarked documents
    • Production hardening: Postgres audit table, persistent vector store, review screen, roles from single sign-on

Iterative improvement as data is gathered

Search logs → contentQuestions that came back empty are clustered and become the next units to write, before anyone tunes ranking.
Reviewer overrides → rulesA high override rate on AI proposals means the rules or tags are wrong; near zero may mean rubber-stamping, so a sample is audited.
Golden set → trustThe question set grows from real searches and reruns on every change to extraction, tagging or retrieval.
Evidence → automationLow-risk finding types move toward auto-resolution only after the numbers show the rules are reliable. Legal and clinical never do.

Product requirements (non-negotiable)

  • Roles are enforced on the server, from sign-in
  • Only approved units can be retrieved; verbatim is never generated
  • Publishing a unit and making it searchable succeed together or not at all
  • Every decision lands in an append-only audit trail
  • Live-call answers within a response time agreed with the contact center
  • Golden set and planted conflicts run on every release

Responsible AI guardrails

Getting knowledge wrong is a compliance and safety issue. These guardrails are built into the proof of concept, not promised for later.

AI decides

Mechanical work only

  • Split documents into units; classify type
  • Link exact duplicates
  • Flag drift, staleness and expiry
  • Scope search to what the role may read; decline without evidence
AI recommends, human confirms

Proposals with evidence

  • Topics, tags, knowledge base and channel
  • Merges, variants and wording updates, with a word-level diff
  • New units drafted from unanswered questions
Human only

Decisions with accountability

  • Approve or publish legal or clinical content
  • Resolve a conflict of authority
  • Grant a role access to a knowledge base
  • Retire a source of record

What is in the demo, and why notebooks

The proof of concept is two notebooks rather than an app on purpose. A notebook is compact, shows every step and its data in order, and reruns end to end in under a minute. It keeps attention on the hard part (structure, governance and single-source reuse) instead of on screens. The review screen and channel UIs come once the data and the gates are proven.
